Almost everyone has experienced it: you're sitting in a quiet classroom or meeting when suddenly your stomach lets out a loud growl.

Although we often associate these noises with hunger, your stomach isn't actually "complaining" for food. The rumbling sound, known medically as **borborygmi** (pronounced bor-buh-RIG-my), is a completely normal part of digestion and occurs whether you've eaten recently or not.

## What Causes the Sound?

Your digestive tract is made up of layers of smooth muscle that constantly contract and relax in a process called **peristalsis**. These rhythmic contractions move food, liquids, and gas through the stomach and intestines.

When these contractions push air and digestive fluids through the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, they create vibrations. Those vibrations produce the familiar rumbling or gurgling noises we hear as stomach growls.

The sound isn't your stomach growling. It is the movement of gas and fluid through your digestive system.

## Why Does It Get Louder When You're Hungry?

If your stomach is always moving, why do the noises seem louder when you haven't eaten?

The answer lies in a fascinating process called the **migrating motor complex**, or **MMC**.

## The Migrating Motor Complex

About 2-4 hours after finishing a meal, your digestive system enters a housekeeping phase. During this time, the stomach and small intestine generate powerful waves of muscular contractions known as the migrating motor complex.

Think of the MMC as your digestive tract's cleaning crew.

Its job is to:

- Sweep leftover food particles into the intestines
- Remove excess mucus and bacteria
- Prevent food from remaining in the stomach for too long
- Prepare the digestive tract for your next meal

These contractions are much stronger than the gentle movements that occur while digesting food. Since your stomach is mostly empty, there's less food available to absorb the vibrations, allowing the movement of air and fluids to create louder sounds.

It's a bit like shaking a half-empty water bottle: the less material inside, the more noticeable the sloshing becomes.

## What Role Does Hunger Play?

When your body begins running low on available nutrients, it releases a hormone called **ghrelin**, often referred to as the "hunger hormone."

Ghrelin is primarily produced by the stomach and has several important functions:

- Signals the brain that it's time to eat
- Increases appetite
- Stimulates the release of growth hormone
- Helps activate the migrating motor complex

This is why stomach growling often happens around the same time you begin feeling hungry. The same hormone that tells your brain to seek food also encourages digestive contractions.

## Can Your Stomach Growl Even If You Aren't Hungry?

Yes.

Your stomach can rumble because of:

- Normal digestion after a meal
- Swallowed air while eating or drinking
- Carbonated beverages
- Gas moving through the intestines
- Increased intestinal activity after certain foods

In fact, many stomach noises have nothing to do with hunger at all.

## Why Is It Sometimes So Loud?

Several factors can make borborygmi more noticeable:

- An empty stomach provides less material to dampen vibrations
- Large pockets of gas amplify the sound
- Thin body types may transmit internal sounds more easily
- Quiet environments simply make normal digestive noises easier to hear

Although it can feel embarrassing, these sounds are a sign that your digestive tract is functioning normally.

## When Should You Be Concerned?

Most stomach growling is completely harmless.

However, frequent digestive noises accompanied by symptoms such as the following should be evaluated by a healthcare professional, as they could indicate an underlying gastrointestinal condition:

- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Unexplained weight loss
- Blood in the stool
- Significant abdominal swelling

## The Bottom Line

Your stomach doesn't growl because it's empty. The sounds come from muscles contracting as they move air, fluid, and food through your digestive tract.

When you're hungry, the migrating motor complex and the hormone ghrelin work together to clean out your digestive system, causing those noises.
